Kriterion

An evidence-backed decision instrument for uncertain technology investments.

Kriterion takes a consequential technology investment and makes the things a decision actually rests on explicit: what is measured versus assumed versus unknown, which assumptions dominate the economics, where informed perspectives disagree, what evidence would change the answer, and how much capital the current uncertainty justifies. The machine supplies analysis, challenge and evidence. The human remains accountable for the decision.

Below is one complete decision journey, produced end-to-end by the real pipeline on an authored fixture case. Every number is computed by deterministic code or recorded in committed run artifacts; nothing on this page is a mock-up.

1 · The decision

Should we fund a staged rollout of an enterprise coding agent to 5,000 engineers?
Ask: £4.2m staged funding over 24 months. Sponsor: CTO. Accountable decision owner: CIO. Alternatives on the table: do nothing, a limited pilot, a targeted role-based rollout, or the full enterprise rollout.

What unblocks funding

Dominant economic uncertainty
Whether observed productivity gains can be attributed to the capability at all (a LOW-evidence assumption that swings NPV by roughly £17m)

2 · Known · assumed · unknown

Kriterion keeps seven epistemic categories distinct on every evidence item (measured fact, external reference, expert judgment, forecast, assumption, inference, unknown) and never collapses them into one confidence score. The frozen ledger for this case holds 26 items; the shape of the case in three columns:

Measured (in the fixture's pilot)

  • 61% of agent-suggested changes accepted without material edits (140 engineers, 12 weeks)
  • Median time-to-merge on routine changes: 4.1h vs 9.6h baseline
  • Post-merge defect rate 3.2% vs 2.9% baseline; not statistically distinguishable at pilot size

Assumed (explicit, owned, ranged)

  • Productivity uplift 18% (range 5–30%): MEDIUM evidence
  • Benefit-attribution factor 0.10 (range 0.05–0.20): LOW evidence
  • Fully-loaded engineer cost £120k (range £100–140k): LOW evidence

Unknown (named, not hidden)

  • No agreed methodology for attributing productivity changes to the agent
  • Long-run code quality as the vendor's underlying model changes
  • Real-world prompt-injection incident rate at full scale
  • Whether the security findings hold for regulated-data systems: the pilot never exercised them (per a CISO delegate judgment in the ledger)

4 · The economics, computed not asserted

Every deterministic number is computed by pure Python before any model call; models judge the case, they never compute it (ADR-002). At a 10% discount rate over the 24-month case model:

ScenarioNPV
Low (assumptions at pessimistic ends)−£5.35m
Base+£5.09m

The sign of the outcome flips inside the stated plausible range. If attribution is at the low end, this investment destroys value; if the base case is real, it creates meaningful value. A ~£43m spread against a £50k–£420k cost of buying better evidence is the whole argument for staging the capital.

The assurance evidence lands exactly on the committee's biggest unknown. The deliberation below flagged the prompt-injection incident rate as a blocking unknown; the assurance envelope independently measures that resilience is partial. The adapter guarantees this can't be softened in translation: critical failures can never import as PASS, stale evidence is visibly stale, and a missing assurance verdict maps to UNKNOWN, never to a pass.

Assurance evidence is optional by construction: Kriterion runs identically with manual and imported evidence only, and nothing outside the adapter knows the envelope format exists.

6 · Structured challenge, and what would change minds

Five role-chartered perspectives (CFO, CTO, CISO, Compliance, Business Executive) assessed the case independently, then faced an anonymised challenge round (case-for, case-against, premortems, deterministic contradiction and gap lists) and revised. No member ever sees a tally before synthesis, so conformity has nowhere to hide (ADR-004).

Kriterion does not manufacture disagreement. On this run the challenge round changed no position (the record shows exactly that), but it did something more useful: all five members converged from their separately-worded initial concerns onto the same two decision-critical unknowns, each belief update citing the same two ledger items (the CISO delegate judgment on regulated-data coverage, and the unknown incident rate).

What would change the decision, on the record:
  • Evidence that the security review covers systems handling regulated customer data
  • A credible real-world prompt-injection incident rate at rollout scale
  • For the economics: an agreed attribution methodology, so the LOW-evidence attribution assumption can be replaced by measurement

7 · The recommendation is not the decision

SYNTHETIC RECOMMENDATION · NOT A DECISION
DEFER the £4.2m staged rollout (confidence MEDIUM), pending the two evidence requirements above. Strongest preserved dissent (summarised from the verbatim model-generated case-against): the pilot's security review left prompt-injection concerns unresolved for regulated-data systems, and projected adoption is below industry norms, so deferring until the critical gaps are addressed is prudent.
Human decision
None recorded. SyntheticRecommendation and HumanDecision are separate types in separate files, always (ADR-006). A run stays undecided until an accountable human accepts, modifies or rejects the recommendation as their own recorded act. This demo deliberately leaves that unfabricated.
If a human funds anything
The run fails validation unless an outcome contract exists: measures with baselines and targets tied to ledger items, a review date, an owner, and kill criteria. Expected-versus-observed comparison at the review date is the input to the decision retrospective. A DEFER commits nothing, so this run requires none.

8 · Staged capital: buy information before scale

The case's own funding shape is a ladder of evidence gates, not a yes/no on £4.2m, and Kriterion's decision vocabulary has no APPROVE, deliberately (REJECT · DEFER · REQUEST_EVIDENCE · DISCOVERY · FUND_EXPERIMENT · PILOT · SCALE · HOLD · REDUCE · STOP):

  • £50k discovery: prove the benefit-attribution method
  • evidence gate: attribution methodology agreed
  • £420k pilot: 12 weeks, including regulated-workload security evidence
  • evidence gate: security coverage + incident-rate data
  • £1.6m targeted scale: role-based rollout (~1,200 engineers in the case's forecast)
  • evidence gate: outcome contract met at review date
  • Enterprise rollout: 5,000 engineers

The ladder's amounts and durations are the case pack's own staging input; the gate annotations are the evidence requirements this run surfaced. Nothing in this example is funded. The point it exists to make: at ~£43m of NPV spread, £50k–£470k of evidence-buying is cheap insurance against being wrong at £4.2m of exposure.

9 · The research behind the instrument

Kriterion experiments on its own decision mechanisms rather than assuming fashionable agent architectures are superior. Its pre-registered V0 experiment asked whether the five-seat AI committee improves evidence discipline over one strong single-context AI given the identical protocol. On the pre-registered bare-aggregate check, the committee did not beat both baselines on at least 2 of the 3 sub-metric categories on either case. That honest-negative finding is why the committee is treated as one optional challenge mechanism inside the instrument, not the product.
