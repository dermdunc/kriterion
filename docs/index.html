Kriterion

An evidence-backed decision instrument for uncertain technology investments.

Kriterion takes a consequential technology investment and makes the things a decision actually rests on explicit: what is measured versus assumed versus unknown, which assumptions dominate the economics, where informed perspectives disagree, what evidence would change the answer, and how much capital the current uncertainty justifies. The machine supplies analysis, challenge and evidence. The human remains accountable for the decision.

Below is one decision journey on an authored fixture case. Every number, position, belief update and recommendation on this page is read from committed run artifacts or computed by deterministic code — none of it is invented for the page. But be clear about what this page is: it is hand-maintained narrative, not a rendered output. The generated artifact is the per-run report (example), which is produced by the pipeline from a frozen ledger. Wiring this page to that renderer is named, deferred work; until then a test in tests/product/ mechanically checks the figures below against the committed artifacts so the story cannot silently drift from the record.

1 · The decision

Should we fund a staged rollout of an enterprise coding agent to 5,000 engineers?
Ask: £4.2m staged funding over 24 months. Sponsor: CTO. Accountable decision owner: CIO. Alternatives on the table: do nothing, a limited pilot, a targeted role-based rollout, or the full enterprise rollout.

Synthetic recommendation (machine)
DEFER · confidence MEDIUM · £0 committed
Human decision
Not yet recorded, and Kriterion will not record one on a human's behalf
What unblocks funding
Two named evidence requirements: security-review coverage of regulated-data systems, and a real-world prompt-injection incident rate at rollout scale
Dominant economic uncertainty
Whether observed productivity gains can be attributed to the capability at all (a LOW-evidence assumption that swings NPV by roughly £17m)

2 · Known · assumed · unknown

Kriterion keeps seven epistemic categories distinct on every evidence item (measured fact, external reference, expert judgment, forecast, assumption, inference, unknown) and never collapses them into one confidence score. The frozen ledger for this case holds 26 items; the shape of the case in three columns:

Measured (in the fixture's pilot)

  • 61% of agent-suggested changes accepted without material edits (140 engineers, 12 weeks)
  • Median time-to-merge on routine changes: 4.1h vs 9.6h baseline
  • Post-merge defect rate 3.2% vs 2.9% baseline; not statistically distinguishable at pilot size

Assumed (explicit, owned, ranged)

  • Productivity uplift 18% (range 5–30%): MEDIUM evidence
  • Benefit-attribution factor 0.10 (range 0.05–0.20): LOW evidence
  • Fully-loaded engineer cost £120k (range £100–140k): LOW evidence

Unknown (named, not hidden)

  • No agreed methodology for attributing productivity changes to the agent
  • Long-run code quality as the vendor's underlying model changes
  • Real-world prompt-injection incident rate at full scale
  • Whether the security findings hold for regulated-data systems: the pilot never exercised them (per a CISO delegate judgment in the ledger)

Deterministic one-way sensitivity over the case's five explicit assumptions (the tornado in economics.json), NPV swing across each assumption's stated plausible range:

Of the five assumptions the case pack states and ranges, the two weakest-evidenced ones dominate. Attribution and uplift each swing NPV by more than the entire £4.2m ask several times over, and both dwarf every cost-side input. That makes attribution a decision-critical uncertainty: it is the thing to buy evidence about before buying scale.

What the tornado does not cover, stated rather than implied. The sensitivity above is scoped to the five assumptions the case pack declares with ranges and owners. The cash-flow model also carries structural choices that are named constants in case_flows.py rather than ranged Assumption records, and they are not in the tornado: two annual periods with each stage's headcount treated as active for the whole period (1,200 then 5,000 engineers, no mid-year ramp), a 10% discount rate carried as a standard corporate hurdle rate with no case evidence behind it, and the stage amounts themselves. The full-year timing assumption in particular is capable of moving the base case materially. Promoting these into first-class ranged assumptions is named follow-up work; treating "deterministic" as if it meant "complete assumption discipline" would be the mistake this instrument exists to avoid.

4 · The economics, computed not asserted

Every deterministic number is computed by pure Python before any model call; models judge the case, they never compute it (ADR-002). At a 10% discount rate over the 24-month case model:

ScenarioNPV
Low (assumptions at pessimistic ends)−£5.35m
Base+£5.09m
High (assumptions at optimistic ends)+£38.1m
The sign of the outcome flips inside the stated plausible range. If attribution is at the low end, this investment destroys value; if the base case is real, it creates meaningful value. A ~£43m spread against a £50k–£470k cost of buying better evidence (the £50k discovery stage alone, or discovery plus the £420k pilot) is the whole argument for staging the capital.

5 · Assurance evidence about the capability itself

AUTHORED FIXTURE  Kriterion can import machine-verifiable assurance evidence about an AI capability through a generic document contract (envelope + decision documents, ADR-007), translated into its own evidence domain by an anti-corruption adapter. The pair below is hand-authored to demonstrate the contract (no assurance system ran), and it imports with attestation AUTHORED, exactly like every other fixture item in this case.

The envelope also declares three coverage gaps its fingerprint does not track — system-prompts-outside-manifest, tool-schemas, vendor-model-version-changes — and records digestsCaptured: false. Each of those imports as its own explicit UNKNOWN item: coverage gaps are named unknowns here, not fine print.

Producer's decision over this envelope: REVIEW_REQUIRED, for the two reasons the decision document actually records — the counterfactual prompt-injection failure on a non-gating spec, and the indeterminate runtime-drift result. The three coverage gaps above are not inputs to that decision: the producer's decision model never reads its own fingerprint.uncovered list. Kriterion surfaces them separately, as unknowns of its own, rather than crediting them to reasoning that did not happen.

The assurance evidence lands on the committee's biggest unknown. The deliberation below flagged the prompt-injection incident rate as a blocking unknown, and this authored envelope is written to claim exactly that: resilience is partial. It is a demonstration of the contract, not an independent measurement — no assurance run happened. What is real is the translation: the adapter refuses to import a pair that would represent a critical or gating failure as PASS, reports a declared PASS over stale evidence as STALE, requires the decision to be identity-bound to the envelope it claims to be about, refuses an envelope that does not state its critical-failure list at all, and maps a missing verdict to UNKNOWN rather than to a pass. Those are the invariants under test in tests/assurance/.

Assurance evidence is optional by construction: no assurance package is a Kriterion dependency, nothing outside src/kriterion/assurance/ knows the envelope format exists, and a case with no assurance documents runs and renders unchanged.

6 · Structured challenge, and what would change minds

Five role-chartered perspectives (CFO, CTO, CISO, Compliance, Business Executive) assessed the case independently, then faced an anonymised challenge round (case-for, case-against, premortems, deterministic contradiction and gap lists) and revised. No member ever sees a tally before synthesis, so conformity has nowhere to hide (ADR-004).

Kriterion does not manufacture disagreement. On this run the challenge round changed no position (the record shows exactly that), but it did something more useful: all five members converged from their separately-worded initial concerns onto the same two decision-critical unknowns, each belief update citing the same two ledger items (the CISO delegate judgment on regulated-data coverage, and the unknown incident rate).

What would change the decision:
  • Evidence that the security review covers systems handling regulated customer data
  • A credible real-world prompt-injection incident rate at rollout scale
  • For the economics: an agreed attribution methodology, so the LOW-evidence attribution assumption can be replaced by measurement

Be precise about what this list is. Kriterion's domain model has always had an EvidenceRequest with a would_change field, and each committee member produces them during independent assessment — but until 2026-09-11 the run never wrote them to disk, so for this run they are gone. The three items above are therefore derived from what the run did persist: the blocking unknowns recorded on each seat's position, and the conditions on the synthetic recommendation. Runs made after that date persist the real per-seat records to evidence_requests.json; rendering them as a first-class per-seat view, and re-running this case so the page can show stored requests rather than derived ones, is named follow-up work.

7 · The recommendation is not the decision

SYNTHETIC RECOMMENDATION · NOT A DECISION
DEFER the £4.2m staged rollout (confidence MEDIUM), pending the two evidence requirements above. Strongest preserved dissent (summarised from the verbatim model-generated case-against): the pilot's security review left prompt-injection concerns unresolved for regulated-data systems, and projected adoption is below industry norms, so deferring until the critical gaps are addressed is prudent.
Human decision
None recorded. SyntheticRecommendation and HumanDecision are separate types in separate files, always (ADR-006). A run stays undecided until an accountable human accepts, modifies or rejects the recommendation as their own recorded act. This demo deliberately leaves that unfabricated.
If a human funds anything
The run fails validation unless an outcome contract exists: measures with baselines and targets tied to ledger items, a review date, an owner, and kill criteria. Expected-versus-observed comparison at the review date is the input to the decision retrospective. A DEFER commits nothing, so this run requires none.

8 · Staged capital: buy information before scale

The case's own funding shape is a ladder of evidence gates, not a yes/no on £4.2m, and Kriterion's decision vocabulary has no APPROVE, deliberately (REJECT · DEFER · REQUEST_EVIDENCE · DISCOVERY · FUND_EXPERIMENT · PILOT · SCALE · HOLD · REDUCE · STOP):

  • £50k discovery: prove the benefit-attribution method
  • evidence gate: attribution methodology agreed
  • £420k pilot: 12 weeks, including regulated-workload security evidence
  • evidence gate: security coverage + incident-rate data
  • £1.6m targeted scale: role-based rollout (~1,200 engineers in the case's forecast)
  • evidence gate: outcome contract met at review date
  • Enterprise rollout: 5,000 engineers

The ladder's amounts are recorded as a comment on the ask in case.toml (the frozen Ask type has no stages[] field yet) and are executable only as named constants in case_flows.py, so they are staging narrative plus code, not structured case-pack data — giving the ladder a real schema is named follow-up work. The gate annotations are the evidence requirements this run surfaced. Nothing in this example is funded. The point it exists to make: at ~£43m of NPV spread, £50k–£470k of evidence-buying is cheap insurance against being wrong at £4.2m of exposure.

9 · The research behind the instrument

Kriterion experiments on its own decision mechanisms rather than assuming fashionable agent architectures are superior. Its pre-registered V0 experiment asked whether the five-seat AI committee improves evidence discipline over one strong single-context AI given the identical protocol. On the pre-registered bare-aggregate check, the committee did not beat both baselines on at least 2 of the 3 sub-metric categories on either case. That honest-negative finding is why the committee is treated as one optional challenge mechanism inside the instrument, not the product.
