Kriterion

An evidence-backed decision instrument for uncertain technology investments.

Kriterion makes what a decision actually rests on explicit: what is known, what is assumed, what remains unknown, which assumption dominates the money, where informed perspectives disagree, what evidence would move them, and how much capital the current uncertainty justifies. The machine supplies analysis and challenge. A human remains accountable for the decision.

  • ev-401 EXTERNAL_REFERENCE HIGH REAL

    The four DORA delivery metrics -- deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service -- are reported as statistically predictive of organisational performance, establishing that software delivery performance is measurable and consequential rather than merely a matter of engineering taste.

    Forsgren, Humble & Kim, 'Accelerate: The Science of Lean Software and DevOps' (IT Revolution, 2018); DORA / Google Cloud, Accelerate State of DevOps Report series · 2018 onward

  • ev-403 EXTERNAL_REFERENCE HIGH REAL

    The SPACE framework argues that developer productivity is multidimensional (satisfaction, performance, activity, communication, efficiency) and that no single metric -- including any activity count or time saved -- validly represents it; measuring one dimension alone reliably produces misleading conclusions.

    Forsgren, Storey, Maddila, Zimmermann, Houck & Butler, 'The SPACE of Developer Productivity', ACM Queue, 2021 · 2021

    Developer experience is characterised along three dimensions -- feedback loops, cognitive load and flow state -- and friction in any of them degrades output. This gives platform investment a mechanism of action beyond raw tool consolidation.

    DORA's 2024 research reports that per 25-point increase in AI adoption, delivery throughput fell an estimated 1.5% and delivery stability an estimated 7.2% -- WHILE the same report found AI adoption associated with INCREASED individual productivity, job satisfaction and code quality.     A controlled experiment found treated developers completed a task 55.8 per cent faster than controls.     A randomised controlled trial -- 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real tasks on their own mature repositories -- found AI tooling made them 19 per cent SLOWER, while the same developers believed both beforehand and afterwards that it had sped them up by roughly 20 per cent. The perception gap is the finding that matters here: self-reported developer time saved is not a reliable measurement of time actually saved. Treat the 19 per cent itself as provisional -- METR has since reported that a replication attempt on newer tools did not reproduce a reliable signal.

    METR, arXiv:2507.09089 (July 2025), and METR's own subsequent replication note. Small n; the durable claim is the perception gap, not the magnitude · 2025

How Kriterion earns trust

Everything above is a view of one record. That is a constraint the build enforces, not a description of good intentions.

One decision state

The evidence, the assumptions, the economics, each seat's recorded position and the decision itself live in one structured record for this decision. Every view Kriterion offers reads that record instead of keeping its own copy of the story, because a second copy is where the two versions begin to disagree. This page is the view where that is checked mechanically, on every build.

Narrative is output, not commentary

Every material statement a decision-maker reads here is stamped with the field it was rendered from, and is re-derived from that field before the page is allowed out. A sentence cannot quietly strengthen, soften or invent what the record says, because the sentence is not written by hand.

Fail closed

If Kriterion cannot re-derive a statement from the record, it refuses to publish the page rather than shipping it with a caveat. A run artifact it cannot read is refused by name, not by traceback. This is intended behaviour: a refusal is recoverable, and a polished page resting on something unreadable is not.

A human remains accountable

The machine supplies analysis, challenge and a suggestion. A named human records the decision, in a separate record, in their own words. Kriterion will not write that one on their behalf, and says so plainly when it is missing.

None of that is needed to read this page or to use the instrument. For the mechanics, the decision records and the architecture notes carry the detail this section deliberately leaves out.
